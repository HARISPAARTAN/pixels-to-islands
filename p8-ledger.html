Part 1: Foundations

Decision

Divide every pixel value by 255, subtract the ImageNet mean and divide by the ImageNet standard deviation, channel by channel, before the network sees it.

Why

This page's reading: a network learns most easily when its inputs sit near zero on one common scale, so that no colour starts with a bigger say than the others and one learning rate suits every weight of the first layer (1.3 explains weights and layers, 1.4 gradients and the learning rate). Toy

Evidence

The six numbers, the means 0.485, 0.456 and 0.406 and the standard deviations 0.229, 0.224 and 0.225, are the same in DINO's code and in SelfPatch's code, and our runs use SelfPatch's. Fact

What it does to training

Inputs between about −2.118 and 2.640 keep the first layer's weighted sums moderate, and with them the gradients of its weights (1.4); the normalisation itself has nothing to learn. Toy

If you changed it

Feed the raw values from 0 to 255 instead, and the first layer's weighted sums become about a hundred times larger, and so do the gradients of its weights: a learning rate that suited normalised inputs would overshoot, every step jumping past the bottom (1.4). Lab 1C shows the same effect with its switch "Inputs ten times larger". Toy

Taught in section 1.1: An image is numbers

Decision

Describe every pixel, patch and image by a vector of numbers, and compare two vectors by the cosine of the angle between them.

Why

Arithmetic is all a network can do, and a vector is what arithmetic works on: it can be added, averaged, compared and moved a little at a time. One vector of 384 numbers can carry many traits of a patch at once, and the cosine sums up how far two such vectors agree in one number from −1 to 1, whatever their lengths. Toy

Evidence

The papers and our code compare this way: DINO's head (Part 3) scales its 256-number vectors to length 1, SelfPatch ranks neighbours by cosine similarity, and our rule ranks every pair of patches in a crop by cosine. Fact

What it does to training

The cosine changes smoothly when any of the numbers changes, so its gradient (1.4) can tell every number which way to move to raise one similarity or to lower another; Part 5's loss, the score that its training lowers, pulls and pushes tokens in exactly this way. Toy

If you changed it

Compare with the plain dot product instead, and length would count as much as direction: a long vector scores high against every vector that points its way, so a network could raise the similarities it is asked to raise just by making its vectors longer, without turning any of them. In Lab 1B, drag a tip outwards along its own line: the dot product grows and the cosine stays put. Toy

Taught in section 1.2: Vectors, dot products and cosine similarity

Decision

Bend every output of a layer with a fixed function before the next layer: GELU in the transformer's MLP and in DINO's head.

Why

Without a bend, any stack of layers equals one layer, as the figure above shows with numbers; with a bend, a stack can draw curves and corners, and depth starts to pay. Toy

Evidence

DINO's authors tried ReLU in place of GELU in their head (Part 3) and saw little change: 69.7 % against 68.9 % k-NN accuracy. That score is measured with ImageNet's labels after training: each test photograph gets the label that its nearest training photographs, in the network's numbers, vote for, and the score is the share labelled right. Fact

What it does to training

The gradient flows back through a bend multiplied by its slope: ReLU passes all of it for a positive sum and none for a negative one, while GELU passes a little even below zero (0.1325 at a sum of −0.5), so a unit whose sum is negative can still learn. Toy

If you changed it

Remove the bend and the network can only draw straight lines, however many layers it has. In Lab 1E, add the second layer and set the bend to none: the lab reports the single layer that does the same job. Toy

Taught in section 1.3: A layer: weights, a bias and a bend

Decision

Train by making one number, the loss, smaller, step by step, against its gradient.

Why

A network has millions of parameters, far too many to set by hand or one at a time. One score that falls when the outputs improve gives every parameter a direction, through its gradient, and all of them can move at once. Toy

Evidence

Which loss matters as much as having one. In an ablation, an experiment that swaps out one part and keeps the rest, DINO's authors replaced their loss, a cross-entropy (Part 3), by a mean squared error, and k-NN accuracy dropped from 72.8 % to 52.6 %. That score is measured with ImageNet's labels after training, by letting each test photograph's nearest training photographs, in the network's numbers, vote for its label. Fact

What it does to training

The gradient of the loss is the only message the parameters ever receive: a parameter moves because, and as far as, the loss changes when it moves. Where the loss is flat, nothing moves. Toy

If you changed it

A different loss gives different gradients, and so a different network. Lab 1C uses the mean squared error; with the plain sum of the errors instead, a miss of +2 on one point and −2 on another would cancel into a perfect score of 0. Toy

Taught in section 1.4: A loss, a gradient and a step downhill

Decision

Divide the scores by a temperature before the softmax: 0.1 for DINO's student network, 0.04 for its teacher network in SelfPatch's code and our runs (DINO's paper warms it from 0.04 to 0.07), 0.2 in our rule's loss.

Why

A smaller temperature makes the distribution sharper, so the temperature is the knob that sets how decided the shares are. DINO divides the scores of its teacher network, whose shares the student network learns to match (Part 3), by a smaller temperature than the student's, so the teacher's distribution is the sharper one. Fact

Evidence

The knob is sensitive: DINO's authors found that a fixed teacher temperature above 0.06 led to collapse, every picture getting the same output (1.6), unless training started lower and warmed the temperature up, raising it over the first epochs. Fact

What it does to training

The gradient of a softmax loss is divided by τ and spread by the shares: a small τ gives large steps while the right option's share is low and almost none once it dominates. Toy

If you changed it

In Lab 1D, choose the teacher's 0.04 of our runs and the top score takes 0.999 of the share; choose 5 and the three shares come close to a third each. Toy

Taught in section 1.5: Softmax and temperature

Decision

Learn from the photographs alone: no label enters training, a setting of a run, the choice of a checkpoint (a saved copy of the network) or the decoder (Part 6).

Why

The thesis asks how well a network can find the objects of a photograph by itself, so the training reads images only and no label enters a loss, a gate (an automatic check that reads a run's numbers) or the decoder. Fact

Evidence

DINO's authors report that features learned without labels contain explicit information about the segmentation of an image, and that this does not emerge as clearly with supervised training. Features are the numbers a network computes for a picture; the segmentation says which pixels belong to which object. In their supplement, the network's attention maps (Part 2), cut at a threshold, overlap human-drawn object masks with a Jaccard similarity of 45.9 for DINO against 27.3 for supervised training, where 100 would be a perfect overlap. Those masks are labels, used only to measure after training. Fact

What it does to training

Without labels the loss has to be built from the images: from two views of one photograph (DINO), from neighbouring patches (SelfPatch), from the most and the least similar patches of a crop (our rule). What the network learns is whatever those tasks reward. Toy

If you changed it

Train with labels and the network learns the categories that someone chose to name; the thesis could then no longer ask what a network finds by itself. Toy

Part 2: Transformer

Decision

ViT-S/16 cuts every picture into patches of 16 × 16 pixels; DINO also trains a /8 version with patches of 8 × 8. Fact

Why

DINO uses patches of 16 or of 8 and states the price of the smaller one in its Table 1: at 224 pixels ViT-S/16 reads 197 tokens and runs 1,007 images per second, ViT-S/8 reads 785 tokens and runs 180 images per second, with the same 21 M parameters. Fact

Evidence

DINO measures its frozen features with ImageNet's labels in two ways: a linear classifier, one linear layer trained on the features to name each picture's class, and a k-NN classifier, which gives a picture the class most common among the k labelled pictures whose features lie closest to its own. ViT-S/16 names the right class first for 77.0 % of the pictures with the linear classifier and 74.5 % with k-NN, ViT-S/8 for 79.7 % and 78.3 %; DINO's authors report that the /8 variants help more than a larger ViT and add no parameters, at the cost of memory and speed. Fact

What it does to training

The patch size sets how many tokens attention compares. Halving it from 16 to 8 makes four times as many patches, and every head of every block scores every pair of tokens, so sixteen times as many scores per head; each token also covers a smaller piece of the picture, so the maps it can learn are finer. Toy

If you changed it

Lab 2A lets you try 8, 16 and 32 pixels. SelfPatch trains each patch to agree with its k most similar neighbours, its positives (k = 4 in the paper, Part 4), and tests the result with labels on DAVIS, a set of videos: the object masks drawn on a video's first frame are carried from frame to frame by matching each patch with its nearest neighbours among the frozen patch features, a test called label propagation. Its supplement finds that the best k depends on the patch's size: 6 for patches of 8 pixels, 4 for 16 and 2 for 32 (DAVIS scores 62.0, 53.1 and 36.5), because smaller patches have more neighbours on the same object. Fact

Taught in section 2.1: Cutting an image into 16 by 16 patches

Decision

DINO adds one learned class token to the 196 patch tokens; the SelfPatch backbone that our runs train has none and keeps its class token in a separate head. Fact

Why

DINO: the token's role is to aggregate information from the entire sequence, and DINO's training head, which it calls the projection head (Part 3), is attached at its output; the name class token is kept for consistency with earlier work, although no label is attached to it. Fact

Evidence

SelfPatch's authors point out that image-level methods such as DINO train only the class token's output, although a ViT produces an output for every patch; SelfPatch adds a loss on the patch tokens and moves its image-level summary into a small head built on class attention, attention in which only the head's own class token asks (Part 4). On DAVIS, measured with labels, DINO scores 60.7 and DINO with SelfPatch 62.7. Fact

What it does to training

When the loss reads the class token's output alone, the patch tokens learn only through it: the gradient flows from the head into [CLS], and from there, through each attention weight, into the patches it looked at, so a patch that the class token ignores gets little signal. Toy

If you changed it

Taking the token out of a trained DINO changes every patch token, because the token took part in every attention step (a conclusion our architecture document draws from the code); Lab 2D shows the last block of the released DINO with its class token beside the converted network without it. Fact

Taught in section 2.2: From patch to token

Decision

Every score q · k is divided by √64 = 8, the square root of a head's width, before the softmax; DINO builds on the standard Transformer and refers to Vaswani et al. for its details. Fact

Why

The reason, worked out by this page: for vectors of d random numbers of spread 1, q · k is a sum of d products, so its spread grows like √d; dividing by √d brings it back to about 1 whatever the width, so the softmax behaves the same in a narrow head and in a wide one. Toy

Evidence

In Lab 2B, 2,000 scores of random 64-number vectors spread 7.80 before the division and 0.98 after it; one row of 196 such scores gives its largest key 0.508 of the weight without the division, as concentrated as 3.6 equal keys, and 0.027 with it; at the width of a whole token, 384, the undivided row gives one key 0.996 of it. Toy

What it does to training

A softmax row that gives one key nearly everything barely moves when its scores move. Its slope is the table of how much each weight moves when each score moves: ai (1 − ai) for weight i and its own score, −ai aj for weight i and the score of key j; for the weights (1, 0, 0) every entry is 0. The table's size, the square root of the sum of its squared entries, shrinks towards 0 (0.007 in the lab at a width of 384 without the division, against 0.111 with it), and then almost no gradient reaches the queries and keys. A row with a few favourites still moves, but only among them: the keys it ignores get almost no gradient. With the division every head starts with a broad row whose gradient reaches every key. Toy

If you changed it

Without the division a head of 64 numbers starts out peaked (the lab's row puts 0.508 of its weight on one key), each token copying the few tokens that the random start happens to favour; divide by more and every row flattens towards an even blend. Switch the division off in Lab 2B and widen the vectors. Toy

Taught in section 2.3: Attention, worked

Decision

6 heads of 64 numbers in every block of ViT-S/16; 6 × 64 = 384. Fact

Why

DINO keeps the default of 6 heads in every experiment of its paper and measured other numbers of heads in its supplement: more heads raise the k-NN accuracy a little and lower the throughput, the number of images processed per second. Fact

Evidence

Measured with labels on ImageNet as k-NN accuracy and images per second: 6 heads give 72.8 at 1,007; 8 heads 73.1 at 971; 12 heads 73.7 at 927; 16 heads 73.8 at 860. Fact

What it does to training

Each head has its own slice of the query, key and value layers and its own softmax, so each can learn to look for a different kind of match, and the gradient of each head moves only its own slice; at a fixed token width, more heads means narrower ones. In Lab 2B six toy heads look at colour, brightness, texture, position and edges on the same picture. Toy

If you changed it

With fewer heads each token would get fewer separate blends. For the dog's nose in Lab 2D, the six heads of the released SelfPatch network look at six different places: the forehead, the face around the nose, the right cheek, the eye and the cat's face, the edges of the head, and the chin. Real

Taught in section 2.3: Attention, worked

Decision

Every attention step and every MLP of the backbone has a skip connection that adds its input back onto its output: U = Z + MSA(LN(Z)), Z′ = U + MLP(LN(U)). Fact

Why

DINO feeds its tokens to a standard Transformer, following the implementation of DeiT, whose self-attention and feed-forward layers run with skip connections around them; it takes the skips with that design. Fact

Evidence

In Lab 2C's stack of twelve toy blocks the gradient that reaches the first block has size 1.3 with the skips and 0.000036 without them, and 100 steps of gradient descent bring the toy task's loss to 0.002 with them and to 0.630 without. Toy

What it does to training

The add copies the gradient unchanged into the block's input, so twelve blocks deep the first block still hears the loss; and because each block only adds a correction, a block that has nothing useful to add can learn to add little. Toy

If you changed it

Switch the skips off in Lab 2C: the token's own numbers are gone after one block, and in the stack the gradient that reaches block 1 shrinks about thirty-six thousand times. Toy

Decision

A LayerNorm with ε = 1e-6 before every attention step and every MLP, the pre-norm order, and no LayerNorm after the last block of SelfPatch's backbone. Fact

Why

DINO feeds its tokens to a standard Transformer network with a pre-norm layer normalisation, following the implementation of DeiT. Fact

Evidence

In Lab 2C at a weight spread of 0.4 the tokens leaving twelve toy blocks average 5.991 with LayerNorm and 45.944 without it; at a spread of 0.5, 9.637 and 1,195.374. At the lab's starting spread of 0.2, 100 steps of training bring the toy task's loss to 0.002 with LayerNorm and to 0.137 without it. Toy

What it does to training

Each attention step and each MLP always reads numbers of mean 0 and spread 1, whatever the earlier blocks did to a token's size, so learning cannot push their inputs out of range, while γ and β still let the network choose a scale and a shift for every number. On the way back, LayerNorm's gradient drops the parts that would only shift or stretch a token and divides the rest by the token's spread. Toy

If you changed it

Switch LayerNorm off in Lab 2C and raise the spread of the weights: the stack's tokens grow block after block, and with them every score inside attention. Toy

Decision

ViT-S: 12 blocks of width 384 with 6 heads, 21,664,128 parameters in the backbone our runs train. Fact

Why

The SelfPatch authors compare ViT-S/16 with ResNet50 because both are conventional choices of a similar size, 22 M and 26 M parameters; DINO's Table 1 lists ViT-S as its small Transformer beside ViT-B. Fact

Evidence

DINO's Table 1: ViT-S has 12 blocks of width 384 and 6 heads, 21 M parameters, and runs 1,007 images per second; ViT-B has 12 blocks of width 768 and 12 heads, 85 M parameters, and runs 312. Fact

Width and depth set the cost of every training step: each block holds 1,774,464 parameters and every head scores every pair of tokens, and in DINO's Table 1 a network about four times larger, ViT-B, runs about three times slower. Fact

Decision

The decoder reads the teacher's last-block keys at 480 pixels; the contrastive rule reads the teacher's last-block output tokens of every training crop, at 224 and at 96 pixels, and the linear probe reads them at 224 pixels. Fact

Why

The decoder follows MaskCut and TokenCut, which read the final attention layer's keys as their patch features; the rule ranks the teacher's final-block tokens by their cosine, as the protocol of 8 October fixes. Fact

Evidence

Our probe reading finds the two measurements ranking the networks in opposite directions: the probe puts the converted DINO network last, 9.4 points of its score behind the released SelfPatch network, while the decoder reads it best. The decoder groups patches by how similar their keys are within one image, while the probe, which uses labels after training, reads how well a linear layer separates the categories from the tokens: the two reward different properties, so the thesis reports both. Reading

What it does to training

The rule reads the teacher's tokens at every training step to choose each patch's positives, the patches its token is pulled towards, and its negatives, the patches it is pushed away from (Part 5); the gradient of its loss flows into every block of the student. The frozen decoder reads the keys only from saved checkpoints, never inside a training step, and they change as block 12's query-key-value layer and the block-11 tokens it reads change. Fact

If you changed it

Keys carry a constant part, the bias bk that every key of the last block receives: in the released SelfPatch checkpoint its length is 28.25 against DINO's 0.041, which is why the decoder subtracts each image's mean key before its cut (Part 6). The decoder's settings were chosen on keys, as the best of 144 settings on the untrained converted DINO network, scored with the labels of a separate set of 1,000 validation images, each opening of which is recorded in the project's ledger; reading tokens would need a decoder chosen afresh. Fact

Part 3: DINO

Decision

Train a student network to match a teacher network that has the same architecture and is built from the student's own past: self-distillation, with no label anywhere.

Why

The authors cast self-supervised learning as knowledge distillation in which no teacher exists in advance, so they build the teacher from past iterations of the student, and they call the result self-distillation with no labels.Fact

Evidence

Without the momentum teacher, DINO's framework does not work: row 2 of Table 7 reaches 0.1 % k-NN and 0.1 % linear accuracy against 72.8 % and 76.1 % for DINO, both measured afterwards with ImageNet's labels.Fact

What it does to training

At every step the gradient flows into the student only; the teacher's answers are fixed targets for that step, and the teacher moves afterwards, by averaging the student's weights.Fact

If you changed it

A teacher that is a copy of the student, or the student of the previous iteration, fails at 0.1 % k-NN accuracy; the student of the previous epoch works, at 66.6 % against 72.8 % for the moving average.Fact

Decision

Cut every image into two global crops of 224 pixels and several local crops of 96 pixels: in SelfPatch's code and our runs the global crops cover 0.25 to 1.0 of the area and 8 local crops cover 0.05 to 0.25; the released DINO run used 10 local crops.

Why

All crops pass through the student and only the global views through the teacher, which, the authors write, encourages local-to-global correspondences.Fact

Evidence

Without multi-crop, k-NN accuracy falls from 72.8 % to 67.9 % (Table 7, row 4). In the supplement, two global and ten local crops reach 74.6 % linear accuracy after 24.2 hours of training where two global crops alone reach 72.5 % after 45.9 hours, at a peak memory of 15.4 GB per GPU against 9.3 GB.Fact

What it does to training

Each image gives 18 loss pairs, 16 of which ask a small crop for a large crop's answer, and a 96-pixel crop holds 36 patches against 196, so the extra views cost far less than extra global crops would.Toy

The supplement moves the scale s that separates local from global crops: 0.08 gives 65.6 % and 0.32 gives 69.8 % k-NN accuracy, best near 0.3. Adding 2, 6 and 10 local crops raises the linear accuracy each time, by less from 6 to 10 at 300 epochs.Fact

Decision

Put a projection head between the backbone and the loss: a three-layer MLP from 384 to 2,048 to 2,048 to a 256-number bottleneck, L2 normalisation, then a weight-normalised layer of K = 65,536 outputs, and no batch normalisation anywhere.

Why

In the supplement's words, as in other self-supervised frameworks, a projection head improves the accuracy greatly, while the features used downstream are the backbone's; the design is inspired by the projection head with a prototype layer of SwAV, and it has no batch normalisation.Fact

Evidence

With the L2 bottleneck, 2, 3 and 4 linear layers give 62.2, 68.0 and 69.3 % k-NN accuracy; without it, 1 and 2 layers give 61.6 and 62.9 % and 3 or 4 layers fail at 0.1 %. GELU against ReLU: 69.7 against 68.9 %; batch normalisation in the heads: 68.6 against 69.7 % without.Fact

What it does to training

The head turns a crop's summary into the scores the loss compares, and the gradient of the loss flows back through the head into the backbone. Whatever is peculiar to the training task can settle in the head, while the backbone's tokens, which later tasks and our decoder read, stay general.Toy

If you changed it

Without the L2 bottleneck, a head of three or four linear layers does not train (0.1 %): the bottleneck is what keeps a deep head stable.Fact

Why

The supplement finds that a large output dimension improves the accuracy, and that the L2-normalisation bottleneck keeps the number of parameters moderate.Fact

Evidence

k-NN accuracy for K = 1,024: 67.8 %; 4,096: 69.3 %; 16,384: 69.2 %; 65,536: 69.7 %; 262,144: 69.1 %.Fact

Decision

Subtract a centre c, a moving average of the teacher's scores over batches with rate m = 0.9, from the teacher's scores before its softmax.

Why

Centring prevents one dimension from dominating, while it encourages a uniform output. It depends only on first-order batch statistics and acts like a bias term added to the teacher, and because the centre moves as a moving average, DINO works across batch sizes.Fact

Evidence

In the collapse study, without centring the teacher's entropy falls to 0 while its KL divergence to the student falls to 0: a collapse. The centre's rate matters: 0, 0.9 and 0.99 give 69.1, 69.7 and 69.4 % k-NN accuracy, while 0.999, too slow, collapses to 0.1 %.Fact

What it does to training

A prototype that scores high for every picture raises the centre by the same amount and loses its lead, so the targets differ from picture to picture and the student has to look at each picture to match them.Toy

If you changed it

Switch centring off in Lab 3E: at the lab's default start the four pictures' answers run into one corner of the triangle, the first form of collapse.Toy

Taught in section 3.4: Centring and sharpening

Decision

Give the teacher a low temperature, τt = 0.04 rising to 0.07 over the first 30 epochs in DINO (0.04 for the whole run in SelfPatch's code and our runs), against τs = 0.1 for the student.

Why

Sharpening has the opposite effect to centring: it pushes towards one dominant dimension. Applying both operations balances their effects, which the authors find sufficient to avoid collapse in the presence of a momentum teacher.Fact

Evidence

k-NN accuracy for a teacher temperature of 0: 43.9 %; 0.02: 66.7 %; 0.04: 69.6 %; 0.06: 68.7 %; 0.08: 0.1 %, a collapse; 0.04 rising to 0.07: 69.7 %. Above 0.06, training collapses unless the temperature starts lower and warms up.Fact

What it does to training

A target sharper than the student's own answer asks the student to commit: the gradient (Ps − Pt) / τs pushes hardest on the prototypes the teacher favours, as the walk of 3.4 shows.Toy

If you changed it

Without sharpening, the teacher's entropy rises to its largest value, −log(1/K), and its KL divergence to the student goes to 0: the second form of collapse. In Lab 3E the answers drift to the middle of the triangle.Fact

Taught in section 3.4: Centring and sharpening

Decision

Match the student's distribution to the teacher's by minimising the cross-entropy H(Pt, Ps) = −Σ Pt log Ps.

Why

DINO presents its objective as knowledge distillation: given a fixed teacher, the student learns to match the teacher's distributions by minimising the cross-entropy with respect to its own weights.Fact

Evidence

With a mean squared error in its place, k-NN accuracy falls from 72.8 % to 52.6 % and linear accuracy from 76.1 % to 62.4 % (Table 7, row 5).Fact

What it does to training

The gradient on each student score is (Ps − Pt) / τs: the scores of prototypes the teacher favours more than the student does go up, the others go down, and the push stops when the two distributions agree.Toy

If you changed it

SelfPatch's paper writes the same distance as a KL divergence; the two differ by the teacher's entropy, which has no gradient, so they train the student identically.Fact

Taught in section 3.5: The loss over the pairs of crops

Decision

8.2 Glossary

Assumes: nothing. Gives: every term the course glosses, with its definition, in alphabetical order.

On every page a glossed term is set in italics and underlined: click it, tap it, or reach it with the Tab key and press Enter, and its definition opens in a small box beside it. This glossary repeats the definitions of all the pages. Where two parts define the same term, the earlier part's definition is the one kept here, and each page keeps its own wording in the glossary at its foot. Fact

Activation
The fixed function, also called the bend, that a layer applies to each of its weighted sums before the next layer. ReLU and GELU are two.
AdamW
The optimiser that DINO, SelfPatch and our runs use: it keeps running averages of each parameter's gradient and of its square, moves each parameter by the first divided by the square root of the second, times the learning rate, and shrinks every weight a little at each step.
Affinity
In the decoder, the table of link weights between every pair of patches of one picture: a full link of one where the cosine of two keys is above τ, and a hair-thin link otherwise.
Aggregation module
SelfPatch's small learned network that summarises a set of tokens into one: a learned query token asks two class-attention blocks, and a final LayerNorm rescales the answer. It makes each anchor's target from its positives, and the crop's image-level token from all of its tokens.
Anchor
The patch whose lesson is being built. Every patch of a crop takes a turn as the anchor, and its target is made from its positives.
AP100
The mask average precision of COCO's evaluation with the object categories ignored and up to 100 masks per image. The decoder's masks are ranked by a label-free score; a mask is a hit when its IoU (overlap divided by union) with a true object not yet matched reaches the threshold, and the precision over the levels of recall is averaged over ten thresholds, from an IoU of 50 to 95 per cent.
arXiv
A free public website where researchers post their papers. Each paper there keeps a permanent number, such as 2104.14294 for DINO, so that anyone can find the same paper again.
Asymmetry
The difference SelfPatch builds in between its two networks: the teacher makes each target from the anchor's aggregated positives, while the student predicts it from the anchor's own token alone.
Attention
The step of a transformer block in which every token takes a weighted sum of all the tokens' values, weighted by how well its query matches each token's key.
Attention head
One of several attention computations that run side by side in a block, each on its own slice of the queries, keys and values, with its own scores and weights. ViT-S/16 has 6 heads of 64 numbers in every block.
Attention weight
One share of a row of attention: the softmax of a query's scores against all the keys, so the shares of a row are positive and add up to 1. They are computed afresh for every picture, unlike the learned weights of the network.
Attraction
A loss term that only pulls tokens together. SelfPatch's patch loss is attraction only: nothing in it lowers the agreement of any pair of patches.
Augmentation
A random change applied to a training crop, such as a mirror flip, a colour change or a blur, so that two views of one image differ.
Average precision
A detection score, AP. The detections are matched to the truth objects picture by picture, a match counting when its IoU reaches a threshold; then, going down all the detections from the most confident, the best precision reached at each level of recall is averaged over the levels from 0 to 1. AP50 uses the threshold 0.5; AP100 averages over the ten thresholds from 0.5 to 0.95 and allows up to 100 detections per image. Our readings ignore categories and pool all the panel's images.
Average recall
The share of the truth objects that the detections find, averaged over the ten IoU thresholds from 0.5 to 0.95. AR100 allows up to 100 detections per image.
Backbone
The part of the network that turns a picture into tokens; in this course, ViT-S/16. The tokens and keys that later parts read come out of it.
Background false-positive rate
The share of the truth background's pixels that some predicted region covers. It is a cost: lower is better.
Backpropagation
The procedure that computes the gradient for every parameter by passing the loss's sensitivity backwards through the network, one layer at a time.
Backward pass
The computation that runs after the loss is measured. It goes back through the network's operations in reverse order and finds, for every adjustable weight, how the loss would change if that weight moved a little: the weight's gradient.
Batch
The images whose gradients are averaged before one step: 256 in our runs.
Bias
A learned number that a layer adds to one of its weighted sums.
Bias correction
The step of AdamW that divides its two running averages by one minus their keep rate raised to the step number, which makes up for their start at zero.
Bias-free keys
A key rule that subtracts the last block's key bias from every key before the decoder compares them. It leaves any other shared part of the keys in place.
Bicubic interpolation
A smooth way to resize a grid of numbers, which estimates each new value from nearby old values with cubic curves.
Bootstrap interval (image-bootstrap interval)
A range for a number measured on a panel of images. Draw the panel's images again at random with replacement, recompute the number, repeat that many times and keep the middle 95 per cent of the results: the interval shows how much the number could move with other images of the same kind. It holds no seed spread.
Bottleneck
The narrow layer of 256 numbers at the end of the projection head's MLP, just before the L2 normalisation.
Candidate
One of the 8 patches in an anchor's 3 by 3 window, among which its positives are chosen.
Centre
In DINO, a running average of the teacher's scores, one number per prototype, updated after every step.
Centring
Subtracting the centre from the teacher's scores before the softmax, so that no prototype can win for every picture.
Channel
One of the three colours a pixel stores: red, green or blue.
Checkpoint
A saved copy of a network's weights, written during or after training so that it can be loaded again later. Our runs write one every 5 epochs and one at the end; SelfPatch's authors published the checkpoint of their trained network.
Class attention
Attention in which only one token asks: the query token attends to itself and to the set it summarises, and it alone is updated. SelfPatch borrows it from the CaiT model of Touvron and colleagues.
Class token [CLS]
An extra learned token that DINO adds to the patch tokens. It belongs to no patch, gathers information from all of them, and its output summarises the picture. The backbone our runs train has none; SelfPatch keeps its class token in a separate head.
Collapse
The cheap way out of a task without labels: the network gives every picture the same answer, so every pair agrees and nothing about pictures is learned.
Colour jitter
An augmentation that changes a crop's brightness, contrast, saturation and hue by random amounts.
Commit
A saved version of a project's code in the tool Git. Its name is a fingerprint of 40 characters that changes when any of its files changes, so it pins down one exact version of the code.
Component
A group of grid cells that can all be reached from one another by steps up, down, left or right without leaving the group. The decoder's mask is the component of the kept side that holds the seed patch.
Contrastive loss
A loss that pulls an anchor towards its positives and pushes it away from its negatives in one formula. For each positive, a softmax over that positive and all the negatives should put most of its weight on the positive; the loss is minus the logarithm of the positive's share. InfoNCE is the contrastive loss of our rule.
Convolution
An operation that slides one small table of weights across a picture and computes a weighted sum at each place. With a window of 16 × 16 pixels that jumps 16 pixels at a time, it applies the same linear map to every patch.
Cosine schedule
A setting that moves from a start value to an end value along half a cosine wave: slowly at first, fastest in the middle and slowly at the end.
Cosine similarity
How alike two directions are: the dot product of two vectors divided by both their lengths, which is the cosine of the angle between them, from −1 for opposite directions through 0 for a right angle to 1 for the same direction.
Crop
A piece cut out of a photograph and resized to a square of a fixed size. In training the network sees crops: usually a part of the photograph, at most all of it.
Cross-entropy
DINO's loss for one pair of crops: minus the sum, over the prototypes, of the teacher's probability times the logarithm of the student's probability. It is smallest when the student's probabilities equal the teacher's.
Decoder
The fixed recipe that turns a trained network's per-patch vectors into regions. Ours is MaskCut with frozen settings; it has no weights and learns nothing.
Defined image
A picture of the panel that holds at least one retained truth object. A reading's pixel scores, their intervals and its regions per image are means over the defined images only; AP and AR pool every image of the panel.
Degenerate cut
A normalised cut that cannot be trusted, because the second and third eigenvalues are equal or nearly so, or the spectrum is broken. The decoder then stops and keeps the masks it already has.
Degree
In the decoder's graph, the sum of one patch's link weights. The degrees sit on the diagonal of the matrix D.
Denominator share
In the contrastive loss, the part of the softmax's denominator that one partner holds: wp for the positive and wn for each negative. The shares add up to 1. The negatives' total, 1 − wp, is the weight of the pull, and each negative's share is how hard it pushes.
Dimension
How many numbers a vector holds: 3 for a pixel, 384 for a token.
Direct push
A push considered for the non-local idea and dropped: it adds the overlap of each negative pair itself to the loss, so that every negative pair is pushed apart, however far apart it already is.
Distillation
Training a student network to reproduce a teacher network's outputs.
DOI
Digital Object Identifier: a permanent name registered for a published document, such as a conference paper. A search for the DOI finds the document even after its web address changes.
Dot product
The sum of the products of two vectors' matching numbers. It is large when the two vectors point the same way.
Eigenvalue
The number λ that goes with one solution v of an equation such as (D − A) v = λ D v. The decoder reads the second-smallest, λ2, and compares it with the third, λ3.
Entropy
How spread out a set of shares is, −Σ p ln p: zero when one option takes everything, largest when all the shares are equal.
Envelope
The file in which a run saves its whole state: the student's and the teacher's weights, the loss's centres, the optimiser's state, the step, the seed and the settings, enough to continue the run or to read it. Our runs write one every 5 epochs, and the last, after epoch 20, is the object of a run.
Epoch
One pass over all the training images: 462 steps in our runs.
Equation walk
The course's way through an equation, in five blocks: the formula, every symbol with its size, a worked example that you can redo by hand, the gradient, and what the term does over training.
Exponential moving average (EMA)
A running average in which each new value gets a small weight and older values fade away step by step. DINO's teacher is an exponential moving average of the student's weights.
Fact
A statement copied from a paper or from our own code, protocols or reports. Its mark names the source; click or tap the mark to read it.
Fiedler vector
The solution of the normalised cut's equation that goes with the second-smallest eigenvalue: one number per patch, similar for patches joined by strong links. The decoder splits the patches at its mean.
Foreground ARI
The adjusted Rand index over the truth objects' pixels. For every pair of these pixels it checks whether the prediction groups the two the way the truth does, scored so that a perfect grouping gets one and a random one about zero.
Foreground union IoU
The IoU of all predicted regions taken together with all truth objects taken together. It ignores which region covers which object.
Forward pass
One computation of a network's output from its input.
Gaussian blur
An augmentation that softens a crop by averaging each pixel with its neighbours, the nearer ones weighing more.
GELU
Short for Gaussian error linear unit: the smooth bend z · Φ(z) that the transformer uses. Large positive numbers pass almost unchanged and large negative ones are squashed towards zero.
Global crop
One of the two large crops taken from each image at every step. In SelfPatch's code and our runs it covers 0.25 to 1.0 of the image's area and is resized to 224 × 224 pixels.
GPU
Short for graphics processing unit: a chip that does thousands of small multiplications at once, which is what a network's layers need.
Gradient
For every parameter, how fast the loss changes when that parameter moves a little. Training moves each parameter against it.
Gradient clipping
Shrinking any gradient that grows past a set size, so that one step cannot jump too far.
Gradient descent
Training by repeated small steps against the gradient: each parameter becomes itself minus the learning rate times its gradient.
Graph density
The share of all pairs of patches that the affinity links, each patch paired with itself included. At a density of one every patch is linked to every other, and the cut has nothing left to separate.
Heat map
A table of numbers drawn as a grid of cells whose shade shows the size of each number. In Lab N the heat map holds the cosine of every pair of the toy crop.
Hinge
A loss that is zero until a quantity crosses a margin and grows after it. The bounded hinge of the first non-local design pushed a pair apart only while its overlap stayed above a margin; it was dropped.
Hungarian matched IoU
The primary endpoint of our readings: truth objects and predicted regions are paired one to one so that the summed IoU is as large as possible, and the score is the mean over the truth objects, an object without a region counting zero.
Hungarian matching
The one-to-one pairing of true objects with predicted regions that makes the sum of their IoUs as large as possible; an object left without a region scores 0.
ImageNet
A large public collection of photographs. Its colour statistics set the normalisation that every picture of this course goes through.
InfoNCE
A contrastive loss: a view must pick out its partner among negatives, scored by the share that their similarity takes in a softmax over the partner and the negatives. MoCo-v2 uses it between images; our rule uses it between the patches of one crop (Part 5).
IoU
Intersection over union: the area two masks share divided by the area they cover together. It is one for a perfect match and zero when they do not overlap.
Island of agreement
A group of patches whose tokens agree with each other and disagree with the patches around them. GLOM pictures the parts of an image as islands of identical vectors; our rule hopes that each object becomes such an island.
Jaccard similarity
The area two masks share divided by the area they cover together: one for identical masks and zero for masks that do not overlap.
k-NN classifier
A simple test of features with labels: a picture gets the label that wins a vote among the k labelled pictures whose features lie closest to its own. DINO weights the vote and lets the 20 nearest take part.
Key
In attention, the vector that a token offers to be matched against the other tokens' queries. The decoder of Part 6 reads the last block's keys as patch features.
Key bias
The fixed vector that the last attention block adds to every key. The network ignores it, because softmax cancels a shift that all scores share, but a decoder that compares raw keys does not.
Key offset
The part that every key of a picture shares, such as the key bias plus the effect of the LayerNorm's shift. Attention cancels it, yet it tilts all the keys towards one direction and raises every cosine between them.
Key rule
The label-free way the decoder prepares keys before comparing them: raw keys, bias-free keys or per-image mean subtraction. It must be the same for every model compared; the owner chose mean subtraction.
KL divergence
Cross-entropy minus the teacher's entropy: a distance between two probability distributions that is zero when they are equal.
L2 normalisation
Dividing a vector by its length so that its length becomes one; the vector then lies on the surface of a sphere.
Lab
An interactive panel of the course: a question, controls and a drawing that changes as you use them. A Toy lab computes a simplification in your browser; a Real lab draws numbers computed once from the real models.
Label
A human-made answer attached to a picture: the name of an object, a box drawn around it, or its exact outline. Training in this thesis never sees one.
Label propagation
A test of patch features that uses labels: the object masks drawn on the first frame of a video are carried from frame to frame by matching each patch with its nearest neighbours among the frozen patch features. SelfPatch reports it on the DAVIS 2017 videos.
Label-free
Learning without labels. In this thesis no label enters training or the choice between runs and checkpoints; labels are read only outside training, with every use recorded: on the validation panel, and once, at the very end, on a sealed test panel.
Label-free decoder
A fixed procedure that turns a network's features into object masks without using any labels. The thesis's decoder reads the teacher's last-block keys.
Layer
One stage of a network: weighted sums of its input plus biases, usually followed by a bend.
LayerNorm
A step that shifts and rescales the numbers of one token to mean 0 and spread 1, then multiplies them by a learned scale and adds a learned shift. ViT-S/16 has one before every attention step and every MLP.
Learning rate
The size of each step, η: how far a parameter moves for each unit of its gradient. In a real run it follows a schedule.
Length
Of a vector: the square root of its dot product with itself, written ‖a‖.
Linear classifier
A single linear map from features to class scores, fitted with labels: a common test of what the features already contain.
Linear probe
One linear layer trained with labels on top of a frozen network's tokens. How well it reads the classes off the tokens measures how linearly the tokens separate them; the network itself does not change.
Local crop
One of the small crops taken from each image at every step, 8 of them in SelfPatch's code and our runs. Each covers 0.05 to 0.25 of the image's area and is resized to 96 × 96 pixels.
Logarithm
Here the natural logarithm, ln, which undoes raising e, about 2.718, to a power: ln 1 = 0, and the logarithm of a small share is a large negative number.
Logit
A raw score for one class, before softmax turns the scores of all classes into probabilities. The largest logit is the prediction.
Loss
One number that scores how wrong a network's outputs are. Training makes it smaller.
Mark
The small badge that tells you what kind of statement you are reading: Fact, Real, Toy or Reading. Click or tap one to see where the statement comes from.
MaskCut
A label-free object finder that links patches with similar keys and splits the resulting graph with normalised cuts, one object at a time.
mBO-i
Mean best overlap over instances: the mean over the truth objects of each one's best IoU with any predicted region. One region may serve several objects.
Mean
The average: add up the numbers and divide by how many there are.
Mean subtraction
The key rule our decoder uses: subtract the average of one picture's keys from each of its keys. It removes any part that every key shares, the key bias included.
Micro-batch
A part of a batch that a card computes at one time. Our runs added up the gradients of 8 micro-batches of 32 images before each step.
mIoU
Mean intersection over union: for each class, the tokens correctly given that class over the union of the tokens that are that class and those predicted as it, averaged over the classes present.
MLP
Short for multilayer perceptron: linear layers with a bend between them. In each transformer block it widens every token from 384 numbers to 1,536 and narrows it back.
Moment (in AdamW)
One of the two running averages that AdamW keeps for every weight. The first moment, m, averages the weight's recent gradients; the second moment, v, averages their squares. AdamW moves the weight by the learning rate times m divided by the square root of v, so a weight with a steady gradient moves by about the learning rate, whatever the size of its gradient.
Momentum
The number m in the teacher's update, just below one, which sets how slowly the teacher follows the student. The centre's rate is the same kind of number: the larger it is, the slower the centre moves.
Moving average
Here, the teacher's weights: after each step every teacher weight moves a small part of the way to the student's weight, so the teacher is a smoothed average of the student's past.
Multi-crop
DINO's way of making views: a few large global crops and several small local crops of the same image.
Multi-head self-attention (MSA)
Attention run as several heads side by side inside one block, among the tokens of one picture, followed by a linear layer that joins the heads' results.
Nat
The unit of entropy and cross-entropy when the logarithm is the natural one.
ncut score
The decoder's confidence score for a mask: one minus the second eigenvalue of its cut, clipped to lie between zero and one.
Negative
A patch that a rule chooses for an anchor to disagree with. Under our rule: a patch of the same crop at or below the crop's low threshold, never one of the anchor's positives, at most 48 per anchor.
Neural network
A chain of layers of simple arithmetic whose weights and biases are learned from examples.
Normalisation
Rescaling pixel values before they enter the network: divide by 255, subtract the ImageNet mean and divide by the ImageNet standard deviation, channel by channel.
Normalised cut
A way to split a graph in two so that the links broken between the two groups are weak compared with all the links inside each group.
Optimiser
The rule that turns gradients into changes of the parameters at each step, such as plain gradient descent or AdamW.
Optimiser step
One update of every weight of the network, from the gradients of one batch of images. Our runs take 9,240 of them.
Overlap
In the pushes that were considered first, oij: the sum over the prototypes of the student's probability for anchor i times the teacher's target probability for patch j. It is 0 when the two share no prototype and 1 only when both put everything on the same one.
Paired contrast
The mean, over the same images, of one checkpoint's value minus another's on each image. Each image is compared with itself, so what makes an image easy or hard for both checkpoints cancels in its difference.
Parameter
A number inside a network that training adjusts: a weight or a bias.
Patch
A square of 16 × 16 pixels cut from the picture; ViT-S/16 turns each patch into one token. A crop of 224 pixels holds 196 patches.
Patch embedding
The learned linear layer that turns each patch's 768 numbers into a token of 384 numbers, the same for every patch; it has 295,296 parameters.
Patch head
SelfPatch's projection head for patches, with the same layers as the image-level head and 4,096 prototypes at its end. It turns a patch's target or prediction into one score per prototype.
Pilot
An earlier run of the project: DINO trained with SelfPatch's own window term under recipe LC0 for 20 epochs. It is run 2's comparator from the same DINO start.
Pixel
One tiny square of a digital picture. A colour pixel stores three whole numbers from 0 to 255: its red, green and blue.
Position embedding
A learned table with one row per grid position, added to the tokens so that the network can tell where each patch came from.
Positive
A candidate that the selection rule keeps for an anchor; SelfPatch keeps the 4 most similar of the 8. The anchor's target is built from its positives only.
Probability distribution
A list of shares, one per option, none negative and all adding up to 1.
Projection head
The small network after the backbone that turns a crop's summary into scores over the prototypes. It exists for training; later tasks read the backbone.
Prototype
One of the learned directions that the projection head compares its output with; DINO's head has 65,536 of them.
Quantile
The value below which a given share of a sorted list falls. The 0.20 quantile of a crop's cosines has a fifth of them at or below it; between two sorted values, linear interpolation takes the point in between.
Query
In attention, the vector that says what a token is looking for; it is matched against every token's key.
Query token
The single learned token that asks in class attention: 384 numbers, the same for every anchor. Its answer, after the blocks, is the summary.
Reading
What we ran and what it showed, quoted from our reading notes and never claimed beyond them.
Real
A number computed once from the real models on real images, stored in the data files of this course and drawn by the page. Its mark names the file and the member.
Recall
The share of the truth objects' pixels that some predicted region covers. It is a credit: higher is better.
Region
A group of patches that the decoder puts together, its guess at one object. Our decoder makes at most five per picture.
ReLU
Short for rectified linear unit: the bend max(0, z). A positive number passes unchanged and a negative one becomes 0.
Repository
A folder of code and documents whose history of changes is recorded, so that any earlier version can be recovered. The paths of this course, such as cluster/train.py, start at the root of the thesis repository.
Residual connection
Adding a step's input back onto its output, so that the step only has to learn a change and the token passes straight through. DINO calls them skip connections.
Seed
The number from which a run draws everything random: in our runs the order of the images in each epoch, the crops of every image, the random numbers of every step and, from the DINO start, the first weights of the fresh heads. Two runs that differ only in their seed differ by chance alone. Our first runs used seed 906; the second seed pair uses 907.
Seed patch
The patch where the decoder's mask starts: the largest entry of the Fiedler vector on the side that is kept, or of its negative when the sides were swapped. MaskCut keeps the connected part of that side that contains it. It differs from the patch with the largest entry ignoring its sign only when the corners swap the sides although that patch lay on the chosen side.
Segmentation
Dividing a picture into regions, for example one region per object.
Selection mask
For every anchor patch, one yes-or-no mark per candidate neighbour: yes where that neighbour is kept as a positive. It has the same shape as the table of similarities it was made from.
Self-distillation
Distillation in which the teacher is built from the student itself: in DINO, as a moving average of the student's past weights.
Self-supervised learning
Training whose task is made from the pictures themselves, so that no label is needed.
SHA-256 sum
A fingerprint of a file: 64 characters, each a digit or a letter from a to f, computed from every byte of it. Change one byte and the sum changes completely, so two files with equal sums hold, in practice, the same bytes.
Shape
The list of a tensor's side lengths, such as 3 × 224 × 224 for a colour crop.
Sharpening
Using a low teacher temperature so that the teacher's probabilities clearly favour a few prototypes.
Softmax
The function that turns scores into shares that add up to 1: raise e to each score, then divide each result by their sum.
Solarise
An augmentation that keeps every pixel value below 128 and turns every value v of 128 or more into 255 − v, so the bright parts turn dark.
Source tag
The short name in square brackets that a Fact or a Reading mark gives for its source, such as [DINO §3.1] or [protocol 2026-10-08 §3.2]. Section 8.3 says which paper or file each tag points to.
Standard deviation
A measure of how widely values spread around their mean.
Step
One round of training: compute the loss on a batch, compute the gradient, and move the weights one small step. Also called an update.
Stochastic depth
A training trick in which the student randomly skips a block's residual branches, its attention and MLP steps, for some pictures.
Stop-gradient
Holding a quantity fixed while the gradient is computed, so that no learning flows through it; DINO applies it to the teacher's outputs.
Student
The network that learns from the loss.
Supervised learning
Training whose loss compares the network's answers with labels.
Teacher
The network whose outputs the student learns to match. In DINO it is an average of the student's past weights and receives no gradient.
Telemetry (progress line)
The numbers a trainer writes about itself while it runs: one line per optimiser step with the losses, the learning rates and the rule's statistics, read afterwards to see how training went.
Temperature
A number τ that divides the scores before a softmax: a smaller τ gives sharper shares, a larger τ flatter ones.
Tensor
A block of numbers arranged along several axes, such as a picture's channels, rows and columns.
Tensor shape
The size of a block of numbers along each of its directions, written with × between the sizes. For a stack of crops it reads: pictures × colour channels × height × width.
Test panel
A separate set of photographs whose labels are sealed and opened only once, at the very end, by one evaluator run. Our contrastive study never opens one.
Token
The vector of 384 numbers that the transformer makes for one square patch of 16 by 16 pixels (Part 2).
Toy
A simplification that the page computes in your browser to show how an idea works. Its numbers measure nothing about the real model.
Toy step
One training step of a lab's toy. The gradient moves the toy's tokens directly, each is scaled back to length 1, and in Lab N the teacher's tokens move a tenth of the way towards the student's. A real step moves the network's weights instead.
Transformer block
The repeated unit of a Vision Transformer: attention among the tokens, then an MLP on each token, each with a LayerNorm before it and a residual connection around it. ViT-S/16 stacks 12 of them.
Truth mask
The outline that a person drew around one object of a photograph, used only after training: by the evaluator, after the predictions are sealed, and by the linear probe.
Twin (LC0 twin)
One of the campaign's two runs of pair 1 under recipe LC0, trained with SelfPatch's own patch term from run 1's start, with the same seed, images, order and crops as run 1: the row twin with SelfPatch's rule, the image twin with the pooled rule.
Unit vector
A vector of length 1. Dividing any vector other than zero by its length gives the unit vector that points the same way.
Validation panel
A set of photographs whose labels may be read, every use recorded in a ledger, to check the decoder and to read trained networks, and never to choose between runs or checkpoints. Ours is the COCO v4 panel of 1,000 photographs.
Value
In attention, the vector that a token hands over; each token's output is a weighted sum of the values.
Vector
An ordered list of numbers, which you can picture as an arrow from the origin.
Verdict (different, equivalent)
The conclusion that a pre-registered rule draws from an interval over several seeds: different when the 95 % interval leaves out zero and the mean difference exceeds a margin fixed in advance, equivalent when the 90 % interval lies inside that margin, and inconclusive otherwise. One seed gives no interval over seeds, so the readings of a single run declare none.
View
One changed version of a training image: a crop with its augmentations.
ViT (Vision Transformer)
A network that cuts a picture into patches, turns each patch into a token and passes the tokens through transformer blocks.
ViT-S/16
The small Vision Transformer with patches of 16 pixels that every model of this course uses: 12 blocks, tokens of 384 numbers, 6 heads.
Warm-up
A period at the start of training in which the learning rate rises gradually to its peak.
Weight
A learned number that multiplies one input of a layer.
Weight decay
A small pull of every weight towards zero at each step, which keeps weights from growing large. A bigger weight-decay number means a stronger pull.
Why card
A box that explains one choice of a paper or of our protocol in five fields: Decision, Why, Evidence, What it does to training, and If you changed it. Part 8 collects every card in its why ledger.
Window
The 3 by 3 square of patches centred on an anchor. Its 8 cells other than the anchor are the anchor's candidates; in SelfPatch's code it wraps round the border of the grid.
Within-object cosine
In Lab N, the mean cosine between two patches of the same object: cat with cat, ball with ball, fence with fence. The grass, the background, is counted on its own. When it rises while the cosine between patches of different kinds falls, islands are forming.
Wrap-around
The official code's rule at the border of the grid: the window continues on the opposite edge, so every anchor has exactly 8 candidates.
Zero-region rule
The evaluator's rule that a prediction with no region on a picture that holds an object gets no credit: zero on every credit and the worst value, one, on the background false-positive rate.
