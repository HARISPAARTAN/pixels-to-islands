The pipeline film

One real photograph, a dog and a cat by a door, followed from its pixels to the regions that the frozen decoder reads out of a trained model, in twelve stops that you can play, pause and step through. Fact

7.1 How to watch the film

Assumes: nothing; each stop names the section that teaches it. Gives: how to move through the film, and what in it is real.

Play runs the twelve stops one after another, and Pause holds the picture. Back and Next, the numbered squares and the left and right arrow keys move one stop at a time, and Home and End jump to the first and the last stop. The speed buttons play at half, normal or double speed. If your system asks for less motion, Play shows the finished picture of each stop and moves on without animating it. Fact

Every stop is also a still picture with its own controls: point at it, click it, or move its slider, and its numbers follow. The panel beside the picture explains the stop, with a mark on every statement. Fact

Every real number in the film comes from the course's data files. Most are run 2's at the end of its 9,240 steps: its teacher's tokens and attention and its student's tokens on the 224-pixel view of this photograph, and its teacher's keys on the 480-pixel view. Stop 12 adds the regions that the decoder finds under the converted DINO and released SelfPatch, and stops 8 and 10 quote run 2's training log at four of its steps. Fact

Run 2 trained our rule from the converted DINO start, with fresh SelfPatch heads, for 20 epochs over the 118,287 images of COCO train2017, without a single label. Fact

Seven things in the film are this page's own, and each says so: the pixel values of stop 1, as your browser decodes the photograph; the crops other than the bundle's view; the scaling of each attention map in stop 4; the shades of stop 5; the flat slice of stop 9; the arithmetic of the AdamW update and of the moving average in stop 10; and the two objects that stand in for a truth in stop 12. 7.3 Where each number came from

Assumes: nothing. Gives: what a SHA-256 sum, a checkpoint and a commit are, and the files, models, training log and code behind every real number of the film, each with its sum.

A SHA-256 sum is a fingerprint of a file: 64 characters, each a digit or a letter from a to f, computed from every byte of the file. Change one byte and the sum changes completely, so two files with equal sums hold, in practice, the same bytes. Fact

A checkpoint is a file that holds a network's weights at one moment of its training. Our runs write one every 5 epochs and call it an envelope: it holds the weights of the student and of the teacher, the optimiser's running averages, the run's settings and everything else that training needs to go on from that step. Fact

A commit is a saved version of a project's code in the tool Git. Its name is a fingerprint of 40 characters that changes when any of its files changes, so a commit's name pins down one exact version of the code. Fact

The data files were computed once by this course's script build/make_bundle.py, on the main processor (the CPU) of the compute server gpu01. The script loaded each model, read its tokens, keys and attention, and ran the decoder with the export package: the copy of our code on gpu01 from which run 2's exports for its readings were computed. The bundle's manifest, a file that lists every input and output of the script with its SHA-256 sum, records them all; the film reads the five files below. Fact

Glossary of this page

AdamW
The optimiser of our runs and of DINO. For every weight it keeps a running average of the gradient and of the gradient's square, steps by about the learning rate against the averaged gradient, and shrinks the weight a little by its weight decay.
Attention
The part of a transformer block in which every token takes a weighted mix of all the tokens. The weights come from a softmax over the scores of the token's query against every key, so they add up to 1.
Attention head
One of the copies of attention that run side by side in a block, each on its own slice of the numbers. ViT-S/16 has 6 heads of 64 numbers each.
Checkpoint
A file that holds a network's weights at one moment of its training. Our runs write one every 5 epochs and call it an envelope.
Commit
A saved version of a project's code in the tool Git. Its name is a fingerprint of 40 characters that changes when any of its files changes, so it pins down one exact version of the code.
Cosine similarity
The cosine of the angle between two vectors: their dot product divided by both lengths. It runs from −1 for opposite directions through 0 to 1 for the same direction.
Crop
A rectangle cut from an image and resized to a fixed size: 224 by 224 pixels for a global crop, 96 by 96 for a local crop.
Envelope
Our runs' name for a checkpoint: the weights of the student and of the teacher, the optimiser's running averages, the run's settings and everything else that training needs to go on from that step.
Fiedler vector
The eigenvector of the second-smallest eigenvalue of a graph's Laplacian. Splitting its values at their mean cuts the graph where it has few links, which is how the decoder finds its normalised cut.
Gradient
How much, and in which direction, a quantity changes when each of its inputs changes a little. Training moves every weight a small step against the gradient of the loss.
Hungarian matching
The one-to-one pairing of true objects with predicted regions that makes the sum of their IoUs as large as possible; an object left without a region scores 0.
InfoNCE
A contrastive loss. For an anchor and one positive it is minus the log of the positive's share in a softmax over the positive and all the negatives, so it falls as the positive outscores the negatives.
IoU
Intersection over union: the pixels that two masks share divided by the pixels that either covers, from 0 for no overlap to 1 for the same mask.
Key
One of the three vectors, query, key and value, that attention makes from each token. The decoder reads the keys of the last block as the features of the patches.
Key rule
The decoder's first step: the image's mean key is taken away from every key, so that what all the keys share does not link every patch with every other.
Moving average
Here, the teacher's weights: after each step every teacher weight moves a small part of the way to the student's weight, so the teacher is a smoothed average of the student's past.
Negative
For an anchor, a token that the rule asks the student to move away from: one of the tokens of the crop least similar to the anchor, at or below τlo.
Normalisation
Dividing every pixel value by 255, then taking away a mean and dividing by a standard deviation for each colour, so that the network reads numbers of a similar size.
Normalised cut
A split of a graph into two parts that cuts few links compared with how linked each part is inside; the decoder finds it with the Fiedler vector.
Patch
A 16 by 16 square of pixels, the piece that the Vision Transformer reads; a 224-pixel view holds 196 of them.
Pixel
One dot of a photograph, stored as three numbers from 0 to 255 for its red, green and blue.
Positive
For an anchor, a token that the rule asks the student to move toward: one of the anchor's most similar tokens in the crop, by the teacher's cosine.
Quantile
The value below which a given share of a list of numbers lies: a fifth of the values lie at or below the 0.20 quantile.
SHA-256 sum
A fingerprint of a file: 64 characters, each a digit or a letter from a to f, computed from every byte of it. Change one byte and the sum changes completely, so two files with equal sums hold, in practice, the same bytes.
Student
The network that the loss trains by gradient descent.
Teacher
The network whose weights are a moving average of the student's. It chooses the targets and receives no gradient.
Temperature
The number that scores are divided by before a softmax: a small temperature makes the softmax sharper, a large one flatter.
Token
The list of 384 numbers that the Vision Transformer keeps for each patch.
